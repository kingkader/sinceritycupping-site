# Dried Bird's-Eye Chili — Lean Export Playbook (Uganda → EU)

*The execution layer for the cofounder brief (`uganda-export-plan.md`). Decision locked: **dried African Bird's-Eye chili (ABE) only** for year one — prove one product end-to-end, keep capital safe, add a brand play in year two.*

**Why this is the right first product:** non-perishable, lightweight, high value-to-weight (low capital tied up, low spoilage), growing EU demand, **out of EUDR scope**, and far lower licensing friction than coffee. The single gatekeeper is **food-safety quality (aflatoxin)** — which means quality is the moat you build, not a cost you fear.

---

## 1. The product spec you're selling to

African Bird's-Eye chili (*Capsicum frutescens*), whole dried red, for the EU spice/oleoresin trade. Target spec to put on your one-pager:

| Attribute | Target | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Moisture | **≤ 10–11%** | Above this → mould → aflatoxin → rejection |
| Colour | Bright/deep red | Shade-dried keeps colour; sun-bleaching loses premium |
| Foreign matter | < 1% | Stones/stalks/leaves get you marked down |
| Mould/insect damage | Visually clean, sorted | Buyers cup/inspect every lot |
| **Aflatoxin** | **Within EU max levels** (verify current Reg.) | The #1 rejection cause for spices from Africa |
| Drying method | **Shade-dried** (not bare-ground sun) | Your quality story + premium vs commodity |

> ⚠️ **Verify before quoting buyers:** EU maximum levels for mycotoxins in spices (*Capsicum*) are set in **Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/915** (aflatoxin B1, total aflatoxins, and ochratoxin A limits). Confirm the *current* numeric limits and any 2025–26 amendments directly from the EU Access2Markets portal or your buyer's spec sheet — do not rely on a remembered figure. This is the spec that makes or breaks a shipment.

**Action:** before you ship anything, send a representative sample to an **accredited lab** (in Uganda or Nairobi) for an **aflatoxin + moisture** test and keep the certificate. A clean lab cert is your single most powerful sales asset.

## 2. Sourcing — paying farmers fairly *is* the marketing

The mission and the margin point the same way: pay a **published, above-market price** for quality-grade chili and put that transparency at the centre of your brand.

**Plan:**
1. Identify **1–2 farmer groups** already growing ABE (Farm Africa and others run chili commercialisation programs — start by asking around those networks, district agricultural officers, and existing out-grower schemes).
2. Agree a **quality-linked price**: a base price + a premium for meeting your moisture/cleanliness/shade-drying spec. This trains your supply base to deliver export grade *and* gives you a fair-price story buyers will pay for.
3. **Don't pre-finance heavily** with your thin capital. Start by buying small, already-dried, spec-meeting lots. Scale buying only once a buyer deposit is funding it.
4. Provide the cheap inputs that fix quality at source: **tarpaulins/raised drying racks, shade netting, moisture-meter spot checks, clean storage bags**. A few hundred dollars here protects every future shipment.

**The data behind "fair pay = good business":** certified/relationship-based African smallholder coffee income runs materially higher than non-certified, and direct, transparent sourcing is exactly what premium EU buyers increasingly want. Your published farmer price is both right and a sales tool.

## 3. Quality protocol (the part that prevents a ruined shipment)

African post-harvest losses average **40–45%** — quality failure on arrival is the #1 reason small exporters die. Own this checklist:

- [ ] **Harvest** fully ripe red pods only (under-ripe = poor colour + uneven drying).
- [ ] **Shade-dry on raised racks/tarpaulins** — never bare ground (soil contact = aflatoxin source).
- [ ] **Dry to ≤10–11% moisture** — verify with a cheap handheld moisture meter, not by feel.
- [ ] **Sort** out mouldy, broken, discoloured pods and foreign matter by hand.
- [ ] **Store** in clean, dry, ventilated bags off the floor, away from moisture.
- [ ] **Lab-test** a representative sample (aflatoxin + moisture) **before booking freight**.
- [ ] **Ship to the sample**: the pre-ship sample is contractually what the bulk must match. Never let bulk quality drift below the sample.

## 4. Finding buyers — a 6-step lean sequence

1. **Target with free data.** Use **ITC Trade Map** (HS code for dried *Capsicum* / paprika-chili, e.g. 0904.21/0904.22) to list the EU countries importing most from East Africa. Germany, UK, Netherlands are the usual leaders.
2. **Build a list of ~30 buyers:** EU **spice importers, oleoresin/extract processors, and ethnic-food wholesalers**. Sources: importer directories, trade-fair exhibitor lists (Biofach, Anuga, SIAL), LinkedIn, and Selina Wamucii/Tridge buyer leads (treat marketplace contacts cautiously).
3. **Lead with a sample kit + lab cert**, not a sales pitch. A clean aflatoxin certificate + a 50–100g sample beats 90% of cold outreach.
4. **Quote FOB Mombasa** (and offer CIF if asked) with clear spec, MOQ, lead time, and price. Have these ready *before* you contact anyone.
5. **Follow up within 48 hours** of any interest; insist politely on **QC feedback** on your sample.
6. **Trade fairs later, on a shared booth.** Once you have a clean track record, exhibit via a national export programme/NGO booth (CBI-recommended) to cut cost.

**Fraud guardrails (non-negotiable):** on marketplaces, **escrow/Trade Assurance only**; never accept or send payment via Western Union/crypto/private accounts; deposit + balance against shipping documents; start small with any new buyer.

## 5. Unit economics — a model you can adjust

**Worked example — one lean LCL lot (~2 tonnes):**

| Line | Assumption | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Buy 2,000 kg dried ABE | ~$2.00/kg (farmgate + fair premium + handling) | –$4,000 |
| Inland transport, sorting, bags, lab test, docs, fumigation, LCL freight share | rough | –$1,200 |
| **Total landed cost (FOB)** | | **–$5,200** |
| Sell 2,000 kg FOB Mombasa | ~$3.75/kg | **+$7,500** |
| **Gross profit** | | **≈ +$2,300** (~31% on revenue) |

- **This fits your £2–10k budget** — and a **30–50% buyer deposit** would cover most of the inventory outlay, so your *own* cash at risk is small.
- It's a **small first number on purpose.** The asset you're building is a **proven supply chain + repeat buyers + a brand**, which is what unlocks bigger lots and trade finance in year two.
- **Levers that move the margin:** higher price for verified shade-dried/low-aflatoxin quality; buyer deposits (free working capital); bigger lots once you have repeat orders (fixed costs spread thinner); cutting the inland/freight share via consolidation.

## 6. First 90 days — the only checklist that matters now

**Days 1–30 — Foundation & supply**
- [ ] Register company + TIN (URSB/URA); start UEPB exporter registration.
- [ ] Visit 1–2 ABE farmer groups; agree a published quality-linked price.
- [ ] Buy inputs (racks/tarps/shade net/moisture meter/clean bags) and set the drying + sorting protocol.
- [ ] Produce a first **spec-grade sample lot**.

**Days 31–60 — Proof & outreach**
- [ ] **Lab-test** the sample (aflatoxin + moisture); get the certificate.
- [ ] Build the **one-page buyer deck** + sample kit + FOB/CIF price.
- [ ] Pull the EU buyer list from ITC Trade Map (~30 targets); start outreach.

**Days 61–90 — First order**
- [ ] Convert interest with samples; negotiate a **deposit** to fund inventory.
- [ ] Execute the **first LCL/shared-container shipment** to the sample spec.
- [ ] Document farmer prices paid + traceability for your first **transparency story**.

**Honest 90-day target:** first lab-certified lot shipped (or a firm order in hand), 2–3 engaged buyers, and a clean quality + fair-price story to scale on. Revenue is secondary to *proof* at this stage.
