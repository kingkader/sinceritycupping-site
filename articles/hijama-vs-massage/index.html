Hijama vs massage: how to choose sensibly

How hijama and massage differ as practices, and why personal medical questions should be taken to a healthcare professional.

Hijama and massage use different methods

Hijama is wet cupping, whereas massage involves manipulating soft tissues. Sincerity publishes wet cupping services and does not list massage, so this comparison explains methods rather than recommending one for an individual.

What hijama involves

NCCIH describes wet cupping as using suction and piercing the skin before blood flows into the cup. The evidence for cupping is limited and the source also describes possible side effects.

Sincerity uses hijama as a secondary name for wet cupping.

What massage involves

The separate NCCIH massage overview describes massage therapy as manipulation of the body’s soft tissues. It is not the same procedure as wet cupping.

Definitions alone do not decide which practice is appropriate for a particular person.

How to choose sensibly

First confirm which service a provider actually offers and read independent information about that method. Sincerity’s published service list contains wet cupping and full spiritual appointments, not massage.

Take personal medical questions to an appropriately qualified healthcare professional rather than relying on a comparison page.

Cupping is complementary care, not a replacement for medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health guide to cupping explains that research is limited and describes possible side effects.
