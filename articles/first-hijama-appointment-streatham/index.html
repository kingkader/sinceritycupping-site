Your first cupping appointment in Streatham: booking and arrival

For a first cupping/hijama appointment at Sincerity Cupping Clinic in Streatham, choose the women’s or men’s service, check the Fresha confirmation, plan travel to 330 Streatham High Rd, and ask practical questions before you agree to proceed.

Start with the right appointment

You do not need to know every term before contacting the clinic. Wet cupping is also called hijama. If you are unsure which listed service matches what you want, ask the clinic to explain the choices before booking.

The services page lists separate women’s and men’s routes. Sister Aisha Mejri sees women and Brother Abu Layla sees men. Standard wet cupping is listed at £45, with 45 minutes for women and 40 minutes for men; the longer full spiritual appointments are £80, with 90 minutes for women and 80 minutes for men.

Check your booking confirmation

Bookings open on Fresha under Sincerity Ruqyah Centre. The about page explains that this is the same centre and the same Streatham address. Before confirming, check the service name, practitioner route, date, time, price and duration shown on the booking screen.

If you cannot find a suitable time, ask the clinic about availability. A WhatsApp message, phone call or email enquiry is not a confirmed appointment; use the Fresha confirmation as the record of your booked time. For payment, changes or cancellation, check the terms presented with your actual booking rather than relying on an old screenshot or article.

Plan your arrival in Streatham

The clinic is at 330 Streatham High Rd, London SW16 6HH, within Sincerity Ruqyah Centre. Use the contact page for the address, map link, phone number and WhatsApp route, and check live directions close to your appointment because transport and traffic can change.

If entrance access, stairs or another practical arrangement matters to your visit, ask the clinic before travelling. This page does not assume that the building or treatment room meets every individual access need without checking.

Privacy and consent come before the service

The practitioner explains the booked service and gives you a private opportunity to ask questions before anything begins. You remain free to pause and ask for clarification before agreeing to proceed.

Useful questions include who will see you, how privacy is arranged, what happens during the booked service, what clothing or access arrangement is practical, and who to contact afterwards.

What does wet cupping mean?

The independent NCCIH overview describes wet cupping as a form of cupping in which the skin is pierced before blood flows into the cup. That general definition does not predict an individual outcome.

Use the published price and duration for the exact appointment you select, rather than assuming every booking follows one timetable.

Evidence and safety

Cupping is complementary care, not a replacement for medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health guide to cupping explains that research is limited and describes possible side effects.

Personal medical questions need an appropriately qualified healthcare professional. The clinic can explain its service and operational policy, but it does not diagnose conditions or direct changes to prescribed care.
