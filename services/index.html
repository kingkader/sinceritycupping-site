What happens

A calm, explained process

Your practitioner explains the appointment and checks your consent before beginning.

  1. 01

    Private conversation

    There is time to discuss the appointment, ask questions and confirm what you are comfortable with.

  2. 02

    Cup placement

    The practitioner places the cups and explains the wet cupping stage before proceeding.

  3. 03

    Clean, single-use setup

    New single-use cups and blades are prepared for each client. Used items are disposed of safely.

  4. 04

    Finish and written information

    The area is cleaned and dressed, then you receive the clinic's written aftercare information.

Clear care boundary

Complementary care, not medical treatment

Cupping is a complementary practice and is not a replacement for medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. We can explain our process and clinic policy, but we do not diagnose conditions or tell you to change prescribed care.

For independent information about evidence and possible side effects, read the NCCIH guide to cupping and safety. Direct personal medical questions to an appropriately qualified healthcare professional before booking.

Our clinic policy

Every appointment starts with an intake conversation. We may pause or postpone an appointment when the information shared means we cannot proceed within our service boundaries.

That is an operational decision, not a medical diagnosis. If the question needs medical judgement, we will direct you to a qualified clinician.

Straight answers

Cupping questions

Before booking

What is wet cupping?

Wet cupping, also called hijama, is a traditional complementary practice. Cups create suction on the skin, then small superficial openings are made before the cups are briefly reapplied. We explain each stage before starting.

Do I need to be Muslim?

No. People of every faith and background are welcome. You do not need a religious reason to book cupping.

Which appointment should I choose?

Choose the card for the client's gender and the service wanted. The price and duration are shown on every card before the link opens Fresha.

At the clinic

Who will I see?

Women see Sister Aisha Mejri. Men see Brother Abu Layla. Appointments are arranged this way to protect privacy and comfort.

What hygiene measures are used?

The treatment area is prepared before each visit. Cups and blades are new and single-use for each client, then disposed of safely.

What if my question is medical?

Ask an appropriately qualified healthcare professional before booking. We can explain the clinic process, but cupping does not replace medical advice, diagnosis or treatment.
